### The Section the Editor Isn't Allowed to Read

- **The news:** Refine, an AI paper-checker, announced partnerships with the American
  Economic Association and the Econometric Society on 2026-08-06
  (https://www.refine.ink/blog/economics-journal-partnerships). Journals use it as a
  "pre-publication audit of the technical execution and exposition of a paper," catching
  errors in reasoning, calculation, and references before they need a correction. It is
  free to journals, "subject to capacity limits." Co-founder Benjamin Golub on the gap it
  fills: "AI made rigorous-looking analysis cheap to produce. A viral math claim, disproved
  within a day, shows why verification can't depend on the right expert happening to look
  at the right moment."
- **Eric's angle:** The detail that makes this a post is in a footnote. Authors see the
  full report. Editors get the technical comments but are cut out of the "Overall Feedback"
  section. So the machine tells the person being judged something it will not tell the
  judge, by design, and somebody had to sit in a meeting and decide that. Defensible
  (an editor should not outsource the accept/reject call to a model that cannot be
  cross-examined) and still the exact shape this site keeps finding: real intent, stated
  plainly, filed where the person with the authority will not see it. Second angle, colder:
  the fix for a plausibility flood the models caused is being installed free into the
  trust infrastructure of a discipline, and free-to-journals is not charity, it is how you
  become the standard before anyone has priced what depending on you costs. Third, the one
  Eric has already measured himself: this is a disposition doing an impression of a rule.
  A checker that usually catches the reasoning error presents to an editor exactly like a
  checker that always does.
