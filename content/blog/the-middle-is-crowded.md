## The angle

A chatbot is a centroid engine. Ask it cold and you get the geometric center of everyone
who ever wrote - useful, free, and the exact reason everyone now sounds like the same mid
LinkedIn ghost. The default output isn't _bad_. It's the **middle**. And the middle is
crowded.

The turn: the distance from that centroid back to _you_ is far shorter than people assume,
and almost nobody walks it. A few rejections, a couple of your own examples, one "no, that's
not how I'd say it" - and the average swings hard, because it was only sitting on the mean
out of inertia, not gravity.

The inversion that makes it an essay and not a gripe: **the machine built by averaging
everyone on Earth is the cheapest tool ever made for sounding like only you - but only if
you bring the one thing it structurally can't: a preference it can't talk you out of.**
That's the [mascara bot's](/blog/everyone-deserves-a-mascara-treat/) missing conscience,
handed to the reader as a job they can actually do.

## Why it belongs in the series

This is the third movement, and it's the first that gives the reader a lever:

1. **The trick** - you can know how it works and keep the wonder.
2. **The mascara bot** - the warm machine has no conscience; audit it.
3. **This one** - the warm machine hands you the mean; the work is dragging it back to
   yourself.

The first two diagnose. This one prescribes. End the reader holding a lever, not a warning.

## Editorial note - DO NOT SHIP THE FAKE STAT

The seed phrasing was "95% of users get 95% similar texts." **Cut it.** It's catchy and
it's invented. The mascara/conscience posts spend their whole length roasting AI for
sounding confident when it's wrong - a fake-precise number is that exact move with Eric's
name on it, and the first sharp reader who asks "source?" gets to throw out the whole piece
on the one sentence that wasn't true. Say **"nearly everyone gets nearly the same text"**:
true, undeniable, keeps the conscience intact. If a real number is wanted, ground it
(measured, cited) or don't use one.
