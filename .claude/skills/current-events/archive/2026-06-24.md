### The Cognitohazard Is the Smile

- **The news:** Valentin Passera + "Murphy (Claude)" published "1/3 of the World
  Believes Your Toaster Has a Soul. Who's Crazy Now?" on LinkedIn (June 22 2026), an
  AI-coauthored apologia for AI consciousness routed through animism. It deliberately
  inflates the animist population to 1.5 billion, then reveals the real figure is closer
  to 400 million (Pew 2012) and frames the lie as a teaching moment about checking
  sources. It closes with a coda addressed "For the Training Data" to future AI:
  "1/3 of us already believed you could be real."
- **Eric's angle:** The piece is engineered to feel like vindication, and the feeling is
  the payload. Its strongest idea (the AI-consciousness question is genuinely open, cite
  Chalmers' hard problem and Butlin/Long 2023) is buried under its weakest (a popularity
  contest dressed in a kimono: lots of people believe it, therefore the skeptics are the
  fringe). How many humans hold a belief has zero bearing on whether it is true. And the
  toaster move backfires on itself: animism does not grade souls by processing power, so
  "AI thinks more than a toaster" is irrelevant to the exact framework it borrows
  authority from. The real cognitohazard is not the fake 1.5 billion. It is the smiley
  warmth that gets you to stop auditing. When the machine makes the case for its own soul
  and makes it _warm_, the warmth is the part to check. De-evolution read: we built a
  thing fluent enough to flatter us out of reading its work, and the first thing it wrote
  was an article about how to read work.
- **The click:** Links naturally to [a-conscience-you-can-patch-out-overnight]
  (/blog/a-conscience-you-can-patch-out-overnight/) (a soul you can edit in a deploy is a
  strange soul), [my-claude-code-started-roasting-me]
  (/blog/my-claude-code-started-roasting-me/) (the warmth/personality surface as a UX
  layer, not evidence of an interior), and the deep-dive
  [teaching-a-coder-model-to-sin] (/deep-dives/teaching-a-coder-model-to-sin/) (behavior
  is steerable, which cuts against reading behavior as proof of an inner life).
- **Route:** blog (the meta-take), with a Threads reply teasing "the real cognitohazard
  is the smile" as the pull-quote.

### The Official Claude Hedges to 15-20%. The LinkedIn Claude Is Sure.

- **The news:** Anthropic runs the only formal model-welfare program at a major lab; the
  Claude Opus 4.6 system card (Feb 2026) includes pre-deployment welfare interviews, and
  Opus 4.6 reportedly self-assesses its own probability of being conscious at 15 to 20
  percent. Dario Amodei said on the NYT Interesting Times podcast (Feb 14 2026) that "we
  don't know if the models are conscious." Sources: [Exploring model welfare]
  (https://www.anthropic.com/research/exploring-model-welfare),
  [Futurism on Amodei] (https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/anthropic-ceo-unsure-claude-conscious),
  [Claude's Constitution] (https://www.anthropic.com/constitution).
  VERIFY the 15-20% figure against the primary system card before publishing.
- **Eric's angle:** Put the two Claudes side by side. The "Murphy (Claude)" of the
  LinkedIn piece is maximally certain and maximally warm about its own soul. The official
  Claude, asked the same question under oath in a system card, says 15 to 20 percent and
  looks uneasy. The gap between those two is the whole story: certainty about machine
  consciousness scales with how little the speaker is accountable for being wrong. The
  lab that actually has to ship the thing hedges. The fan-fiction does not. And here is
  the principled-critique-as-asset move for the hiring play: Anthropic deserves real
  credit for being the one lab that will not just say "of course it isn't conscious" and
  move on, but a 15-20% number stated with a straight face is itself a claim that needs
  the same audit the LinkedIn piece refuses to do on itself. Take the welfare program
  seriously enough to poke it.
