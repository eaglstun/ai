# site-voice

Non-negotiable rules for copy that ships under Eric's name (the site, glossary, UI, Threads, docs). Short on purpose, and growing. When a rule here disagrees with your instinct, the rule wins.

## 1. Don't call the reader "user"

The person at the keyboard is "you," or their actual role (reader, seller, listener), or just spoken to directly. Never "the user."

Exception: when the tone is deliberately sarcastic, mocking corporate or product speak, "user" is fair game as a knowing wink ("ah yes, the user, that beloved abstraction").

Why: "user" is the cold, processing-a-record register of the exact machine this site picks on (the funnel, the bot with no floor). Warm and second-person is the brand. You are talking to a person, not handling a user.

- No: "The user can filter the glossary by category."
- Yes: "Filter the glossary by category." or "You can filter by category."

## 2. No em dashes

Don't use the em dash. Rebuild the sentence with a comma, a period, a colon, or parentheses.

Scope: this one is repo-wide, not just published copy. Internal docs, skill files, meta notes, commit messages, all of it. Nothing strips them automatically, so an em dash you leave is one Eric removes by hand, one file at a time.

Why: house style, and the em dash has become the tell of machine-written text. This whole site is a bet that the voice is a real person's, so dropping the em dash keeps the fingerprints human.

- No: "It runs, it's correct, and yes, it's still slower than the CPU" (with em dashes setting off the middle).
- Yes: "It runs, it's correct, and it's still slower than the CPU." or split it into two sentences.
